The search: the owner asks the Quant Lab in one sentence in its room or with one button. It takes every bot model that has a backtest setup (19 today) and every market with enough price history; for each pair it tries a small grid of settings on the earlier part of the history; it ranks the combinations on a later period the search never saw; the top few start as paper clones in the Quant Lab. The room shows the progress and the time left.
It is a research lab, so it remembers: every combination tried (bot model, market, settings, period) is kept with its result; a new search skips what was already tried on the same data and tries it again only when there is new data; the owner can open the list of what was tested and what came of it.
The check on the held-back period is held by the platform and never shown to the search, and it counts every combination tried (from that memory): the more combinations, the higher the bar, so most "winners" are thrown out (your own condition). A result shows its sign, its number of trades and the number of combinations tried, and is never called a win while it lost (today's clone loop passed a change that only lost less: 2 positions -6.82%, 1 position -3.94%, last 15 days, at least 3 trades).
The Experiments page (/company/app/experiments): on top how many paper bots run now and how many the plan allows (a paid plan: 100 trading, 200 in all; today's "Paper bots at once" goes only from 1 to 10, so raise it to the plan's cap). Per bot: its profitability (return, P&L, closed trades, drawdown, as today, plus the held-back result it was chosen on) and a link that opens its configuration (bot model, market, skills and algorithms, settings); best return first. Going live goes through the Trading desk for the owner's approval, as today: never automatic, no funds move.
