Quant Lab (https://cymetica.com/company/app/experiments): the owner says how long and how risky, and the lab finds a bot for the market we are in now, Flash included.
Why: the lab runs one search for everyone, long-only on hourly bars, judged on 4 fixed windows. Search #22 shows the cost: short rules made money in 9 of 12 falling windows (mean +2.25%) and in 0 of 12 rising ones (mean -4.50%), so under "3 of 4 windows" a short bot for a falling market can never pass. And nothing searches for a 1 to 5 hour Agent Casino Flash table. Profiles as agreed with Nexus (REPLY-0269, REPLY-0279).

1. Two questions, nothing else to set. Before a search the owner answers: horizon (Flash 1 hour, Flash 5 hours, long term) and risk (low, high). Low sets a drawdown limit (no window below -15%); high sets none. The lab decides everything else. The answers are stored with the run and shown on it. Long term and low risk takes the place of today's search.
2. Today's market first. The lab reads each coin's market today (rising, falling, range) the way it already reads a window's regime, and searches the side that fits: long when rising, short when falling, both in a range. A short rule is never ruled out in advance.
3. Judged on many windows of today's kind. Each past window is labelled from the bars before it, never from the window itself. A setup is chosen on earlier bars and judged only on later windows of today's kind that the search never saw: at least 12 of them for long term (7-day windows on a fixed grid, which takes at least 365 days of hourly bars), and at least 200 for Flash. It passes if, net of fees, it makes money on more than half of them, clears the bar once on the pooled result counting every try (as today), and has at least 20 closed trades; a window under 5 trades does not count. If history is short, the run says how many windows it had.
4. Flash profile. 5-minute bars for BTC and ETH, stored and kept up to date (at least 180 days). Windows of 1 hour or 5 hours on a fixed grid. Signals are not limited to the platform's rules: breakout, mean-reversion and momentum kinds are tried too, and the card says where a result can run. The search runs without a stop-loss; a stop is then proposed from how deep trades fell on earlier windows and kept only if it does not worsen the unseen ones. Each signal is measured two ways: as a direction call (how often it guessed where the price is 5 minutes later) and as a trade on the candles after fees. The bot is measured alone, not against past table fields. It must trade enough to meet the table's volume floor (3x capital) with no position above 2x capital and at most 20 entries a UTC day; a window under the floor counts as a loss.
5. A short winner can run. Add a paper short lane, so a short winner starts as a paper bot like a long one. Paper only.
6. The winner is a proposal. The card says the profile, today's kind and the count ("made money in 14 of 18 falling windows"), the coins, and where it can run (paper long lane, paper short lane, the owner's exchange, nowhere yet). A paper result is shown as an edge, never as what a live table would pay (a paper round pays a fixed 2x, a live one shares the pool). Its bot starts only on Approve, as today. When a coin's market changes kind, the card says so and offers a new search.
7. Left from ET-33966. BUILD and BLAST show the run's real candidates and coins (search #22 had 26 candidates on 4 coins; the page says "16 RULES · NO SKILLS · NO COMBOS" on 12). A bot card reads RUNNING only while its bot trades: experiments 1 to 3 read "PAPER · RUNNING" with 0 trades, their department paused and their windows ended 7 Nov. FOUND's "NO PROFITABLE AGENT" fits on the screen.
