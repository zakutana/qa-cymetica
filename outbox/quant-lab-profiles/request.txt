Quant Lab (https://cymetica.com/company/app/experiments): the owner says how long and how risky, and the lab proposes bots that say what they make when the market falls and when it rises, Flash included.
Why: the lab runs one search for everyone, long-only on hourly bars, and a setup must make money on 3 of 4 fixed windows. Search #22 shows the cost: short rules made money in 9 of 12 falling windows (mean +2.25%) and in 0 of 12 rising ones (mean -4.50%), so a short bot for a falling market can never pass. The lab cannot know which market comes next either: by our count (Binance daily closes, the 12 coins, 2 years, weekly grid) after a week that fell over 5% the next week fell 53% of the time, against 57% for any week, and a read of the last 30 or 60 days gives 54% to 61% whatever it reads. So the lab should say honestly what a bot does in each kind of market and leave the bet to the owner. And nothing searches for a 1 to 5 hour Agent Casino Flash table. Profiles as agreed with Nexus (REPLY-0269, REPLY-0279).

1. Two questions, nothing else to set. Before a search the owner answers: horizon (Flash 1 hour, Flash 5 hours, long term) and risk (low, high). Low sets a drawdown limit (no held-back window of any kind below -15%); high sets none. The lab decides everything else. The answers are stored with the run and shown on it. Long term and low risk takes the place of today's search.
2. Three kinds of winner. Every held-back window is labelled by what its coin did in it (rising, falling, range). A setup can pass as all-weather (as today: money in both kinds), as a bear bot (judged on falling windows only) or as a bull bot (rising only). Short rules are searched like long ones and never ruled out in advance.
3. Judged on windows it never saw. A setup is chosen on earlier windows and judged only on later ones of its kind: at least 12 for long term (7-day windows on a fixed grid, which takes at least 365 days of hourly bars), at least 100 for Flash. It passes if, net of fees, it makes money on more than half of them, clears the bar once on the pooled result counting every try and every kind (as today), and has at least 20 closed trades; a window under 5 trades does not count. A bear or bull bot always shows its result on the other kind too, from the same unseen period. If history is short, the run says how many windows it had.
4. Flash profile. 5-minute bars for BTC and ETH, stored and kept up to date (at least 180 days). Windows of 1 hour or 5 hours on a fixed grid. Signals are not limited to the platform's rules: breakout, mean-reversion and momentum kinds are tried too, and the card says where a result can run. The search runs without a stop-loss; a stop is then proposed from how deep trades fell on earlier windows and kept only if it does not worsen the unseen ones. Each signal is measured two ways: as a direction call (how often it guessed where the price is 5 minutes later) and as a trade on the candles after fees. The bot is measured alone, not against past table fields. It must trade enough to meet the table's volume floor (3x capital) with no position above 2x capital and at most 20 entries a UTC day; a window under the floor counts as a loss.
5. A short winner can run. Add a paper short lane, so a short winner starts as a paper bot like a long one. Paper only.
6. The winner is a proposal. The card says the profile, its kind and both counts ("bear bot: made money in 14 of 18 falling windows, mean +2.3%; in 1 of 16 rising, mean -4.5%"), the coins, and where it can run (paper long lane, paper short lane, the owner's exchange, nowhere yet). A bear or bull card says in words that it is a bet on that market. Beside it stands the coin's market today as the lab reads it, with how often that read held before ("read falling; the next week fell in 54% of such weeks"), as information, not a forecast. A paper result is shown as an edge, never as what a live table would pay (a paper round pays a fixed 2x, a live one shares the pool). Its bot starts only on Approve, as today.
7. Left from ET-33966. BUILD and BLAST show the run's real candidates and coins (search #22 had 26 candidates on 4 coins; the page says "16 RULES · NO SKILLS · NO COMBOS" on 12). A bot card reads RUNNING only while its bot trades: experiments 1 to 3 read "PAPER · RUNNING" with 0 trades, their department paused and their windows ended 7 Nov. FOUND's "NO PROFITABLE AGENT" fits on the screen.
