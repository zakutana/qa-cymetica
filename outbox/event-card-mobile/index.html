Connect the dots. On 25 Sep 2026 Novo Nordisk (Wegovy, Ozempic) licensed Nanexa's PharmaShell for up to five monthly/quarterly injectable programs — €615M upfront plus development and regulatory milestones, €1.165B in total; Nanexa's shares rose ~150%. PharmaShell grows an ultra-thin inorganic shell around each drug particle by atomic layer deposition, and Nanexa says that shell is primarily aluminium oxide. Novo is the buyer, so the move is not in Novo — it is in the inorganic-coating supply chain. Given the article's own sentence describing that coating, the Tuatara vector engine ranked Sasol (SSL) first by a wide margin (79% of the total score): Sasol is a merchant specialist in high-purity and ultra-high-purity aluminas (up to 99.999%, made by aluminium-alkoxide chemistry at Brunsbüttel, where it committed €60M in June 2026 to expand capacity), with alumina coatings a named application. No supply contract between Sasol and Nanexa or Novo is documented — this is the thematic read-through, and the report says so.
