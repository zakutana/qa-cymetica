Quant Lab search (https://cymetica.com/company/app/experiments): make a search show whether a setup makes money when the market turns, not on one stretch of the market. The lab decides all of this itself; there is no setting for the owner to choose.
Why: a search uses 90 days and one 60/40 cut, so every blind test is the same bet on the same turn. By our count from Binance hourly closes, in searches #3, #10 and #15 the search period rose on 12 of 12 coins (mean +45% to +48%) and the held-back period fell on 12 of 12 (mean -14% to -18%). All 25 blind results so far lost (#3, #10, #15, #17, #18). Skipping failed setups and adding new settings changed the shortlist, not the outcome.

1. Longer history, several windows. A search uses at least 365 days of hourly bars (or all the price feed has, and says how much) and at least 4 pairs of search period and held-back period stepping through it. At least one held-back window has the 12-coin average falling and at least one has it rising.
2. A setup passes only if it clears the bar (counting every combination ever tried, as today) on more than half of the windows AND its average held-back return across windows is above 0. Money made, not "less lost than the coin": a bot that never trades must not pass.
3. The shortlist is not the top five by search-period gain. Each window takes the best setups per model and per kind of market (rising, falling, range, read from that window's search period only). Short (Red) rules are tested on the same windows. A short rule that passes starts a paper bot, or its card says in one line why it cannot (the spot lane is long-only today).
4. Honest by construction. Everything the lab used to choose (memory, market read) is dated before the first held-back bar of that window and stored with the run. A setup that failed a held-back window is never tested on that same window again.
5. Skills and Named Combinations from /adm are candidates in the real search (ET-33142), after 1 to 4.
6. Show it. Each run in RUNS shows "N of M windows passed", each window's dates and its 12-coin move, and which models and which side (long or short) were shortlisted. A search that could not load a market names it: search #17 covered 10 of 12 markets (BTC and ATOM had 0 bars) and nothing on the page said so.
Keep what stays true: the page and the owner API keep working as today; fields are added, none removed.

[ ] 2 A run reads "N of M windows passed" with the average held-back return; a setup with an average loss never reads as passed.

[ ] 4 The run record shows the date of every input of the choice; a repeat run does not blind-test a setup on a window it already failed.
[ ] 5 A run lists skills and Named Combinations among its candidates.
[ ] 6 RUNS shows the windows and any market that could not be loaded.
