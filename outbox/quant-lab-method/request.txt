Quant Lab search (https://cymetica.com/company/app/experiments): make a search show whether a setup makes money when the market turns, not on one stretch of the market. The lab decides all of this itself; there is no setting for the owner to choose.
Why: a search uses 90 days and one 60/40 cut, so every blind test is the same bet on the same turn. By our count from Binance hourly closes, in searches #3, #10 and #15 the search period rose on 12 of 12 coins (mean +45% to +48%) and the held-back period fell on 12 of 12 (mean -14% to -18%). All 25 blind results so far lost (#3, #10, #15, #17, #18). What ET-33846 shipped (the 36-day skip, ranking by past failures, new settings, the 7-day regime read) changed the shortlist, not the outcome.

1. Several windows. A search holds back at least 4 windows of 36 days each. They never overlap and sit on a fixed grid, so a repeat search is judged on the same windows. Each has its own 54-day search period before it, which takes 199 days of hourly bars; if the price feed has less for a market, the run says how much. The windows must hold both a falling and a rising market; if the newest 4 do not, older ones are added. On Binance closes the newest 4 moved -16%, +15%, +24% and -4% (12-coin mean), so they do today.
2. A setup passes only if, net of the fee and slippage every replay already pays, it (a) makes money on at least 3 of 4 windows (three quarters if there are more), (b) clears the bar once on its result over all windows together, counting every combination ever tried, as today, and (c) has at least 20 closed trades across them. Money made, not "less lost than the coin": a bot that never trades cannot pass. The bar is applied once because per window it would ask about +11% on each of three windows where the pooled test asks about +22% in all (sd 3.23 per trade from /how-it-fits, z 4.47).
3. The shortlist is not the top five by search-period gain. Each window takes the best setups per model and per kind of market (rising, falling, range, read from that window's search period only). Short (Red) rules are tested on the same windows. A short rule that passes starts a paper bot, or its card says in one line why it cannot (the spot lane is long-only today).
4. Honest by construction. Everything the lab used to choose (memory, market read) is dated before the first held-back bar of that window and stored with the run. The 36-day skip stays, counted per window: a setup that failed one window is not tested on that window again and stays free to be tested on the others.
5. Skills and Named Combinations from /adm are candidates in the real search (ET-33142), after 1 to 4.
6. Show it. Each run in RUNS shows "N of M windows made money", the pooled result against the bar, the closed trades, each window's dates, and which models and which side (long or short) were shortlisted. Beside a setup's result on each window stands the same coins held at equal weight ("made +3%, market -14%"), as information, not as the pass rule. A search that could not load a market names it: search #17 covered 10 of 12 markets and nothing on the page said so.
Keep what stays true: the page and the owner API keep working as today; fields are added, none removed.

Done when:

[ ] 2 A run reads "N of M windows made money" with the pooled result, the bar and the closed trades. A setup under 20 trades, losing on more than 1 of 4 windows or under the pooled bar never reads as passed.

[ ] 5 A run lists skills and Named Combinations among its candidates.
