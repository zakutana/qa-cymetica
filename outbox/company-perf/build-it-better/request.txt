Build a Company (/company/app) on a weak computer: a performance package, ready to apply. The owner opened /company/app on a Ryzen 5 5500U laptop (8 GB, AMD Radeon graphics with 496 MB): it loaded about 70% longer than on his desktop, the entry page loaded a 3D office he had not asked for, turning the Universe stuttered, and the office loaded slowly and stuttered. Spec with every number: https://github.com/zakutana/qa-cymetica/blob/073fc16dade7f7fc96324559b14f6c0f22526252/outbox/company-perf/SPEC.md . The patch against your live files (v=6adb7183): https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zakutana/qa-cymetica/073fc16dade7f7fc96324559b14f6c0f22526252/outbox/company-perf/company-perf.patch . The entry page's three pictures: https://github.com/zakutana/qa-cymetica/tree/073fc16dade7f7fc96324559b14f6c0f22526252/outbox/company-perf/new .
Apply it as it is: company-perf.patch applied to your live static/company files gives byte for byte the files we measured (it adds quality.js and changes u3d.js, app.js, start.js and three stylesheets); the three pages' template edits are listed in the spec's section 4 (and in pages.diff); put the three pictures into /static/company/; raise ?v=. Nothing in the API or the data changes. At high the 3D draws the same picture as today (pixel-checked).
A. quality.js (new): one level of 3D per computer, high, medium or low, chosen before anything 3D is built: the graphics chip's name gives the first guess (the owner's "AMD Radeon(TM) Graphics" is low, add-in cards high), the frames correct it (a scene that cannot hold ~38 fps while turned draws fewer pixels, and the next visit starts a level lower), ?q=low|medium|high and Settings > Graphics choose by hand.
B. Build only what is shown: the entry page shows pictures of its scenes at once (no loading screen; at low no 3D engine at all, at high and medium the live scenes come over the pictures after the page, at 30 fps); the 3D office is built when Headquarters opens, not behind the Universe; below high the Universe and the Add-department dialog are built when opened, not in the background on two more WebGPU devices.
C. Draw what the computer can: on a weak computer a frame of the office is the processor's (three.js sets up 1,272 draws one by one), so below high the sun's shadow is drawn 4 times a second or once a second instead of every frame (361 draws), the floors' mirror is a third of the screen or none (80 draws), a corridor's dashes are one mesh; then a pixel budget, no multisampling at low, 30 fps while nobody touches the scene, and no frosted glass (backdrop-filter) over moving 3D.
D. Small: GTAONode.js was imported for code nobody calls (removed); one WebGL probe instead of two.
Measured on a weak machine (2-CU Radeon, processor slowed 3x), today -> at low, which Auto chose there: the entry page on screen 7.7 -> 0.8 s; an office turned 39 -> 60 frames a second (signed in 36 -> 53); signed in, in the 20 s after the office shows, the main thread held 4.0 s -> 0 and the office at 21 -> 30 frames a second; GPU textures 389 -> 142 MB. Not shortened: an office's own loading (9.4 -> 8.3 s). About 70 % of it is three.js building shader code, 113 builds for 70 different vertex-and-fragment pairs; the spec's sections 1 and 7 say where.
Left to you: the levels' numbers are one table in quality.js and can be tuned; nothing about the look at high is left open.
